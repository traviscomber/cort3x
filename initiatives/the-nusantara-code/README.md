# The Nusantara Code: Indonesia's Carbon Pricing Governance Initiative

## Overview

The Nusantara Code represents Indonesia's comprehensive framework for carbon pricing governance and carbon market development. This initiative positions Indonesia as a key player in the global carbon market, leveraging its significant natural carbon credit potential while establishing robust governance structures for carbon trading.

**Last Updated:** November 2025  
**Authority:** Ministry of Environment / Environmental Protection Agency, Republic of Indonesia  
**Deputy:** Climate Change and Carbon Governance

---

## Executive Summary

Indonesia has taken significant steps to strengthen the supply side of its carbon market, supported by a continuously evolving policy framework and active participation from various stakeholders. The country is positioning itself to become a carbon market hub in the ASEAN region, with particular emphasis on:

- **Blue Carbon Potential**: Indonesia is an archipelagic country with the second longest coastline in the world
- **Cross-sectoral Collaboration**: Between government, private sector, and community
- **Market Integrity**: Building long-term trust through transparent regulations
- **International Cooperation**: Bilateral agreements and mutual recognition frameworks

## Carbon Market Harmonization

### Importance of Carbon Markets

Carbon markets play a crucial role in achieving climate targets through:

1. **Flexible and cost-effective** emission reduction mechanisms
2. **Mobilizing financial resources** for climate action
3. **Framework for businesses** to participate in climate solutions
4. **Cooperation between countries** and technology/financial transfer
5. **Efficient carbon markets** encouraging green investment and innovation

### Strong Carbon Credit Regulations

To ensure market integrity, regulations must guarantee:

1. **Environmental Integrity**: Real, measurable emission reductions
2. **Market Credibility and Investor Confidence**: Transparent, reliable systems
3. **Preventing Double Counting**: Robust tracking mechanisms
4. **Ensuring Social and Environmental Safeguards**: Community protection
5. **Supporting Climate Goals**: Alignment with Paris Agreement targets

### Market Harmonization Strategy

**Objectives:**
- Harmonize carbon credit regulations, standards, and methodologies across various frameworks
- Create a more consistent and integrated approach to carbon markets
- Promote interoperability and mutual recognition between SPEI (Sistem Perdagangan Emisi Indonesia) and other certification standards

**JCM Scheme Transformation:**
- Evolution from Kyoto Protocol Regime/Clean Development Mechanism (CDM) to Article 6.2 of the Paris Agreement
- Transition of Independent Crediting Programs (ICPs) to Indonesia Carbon Crediting Scheme
- Standards alignment: VERRA → SPEI, Gold Standard, Plan Vivo, etc.
- **Bilateral Indonesia-Japan Joint Crediting Mechanism** as flagship program

## Role of Carbon Pricing in Environmental Policy

Carbon pricing serves as a powerful policy instrument with multiple functions:

### 1. Emissions Accountability

Every unit of carbon produced must be properly recorded and monitored through the **SRN PPI** (National Registry System). This ensures accountability for emissions from various sectors.

### 2. Incentives and Disincentives

Carbon pricing provides economic incentives for companies investing in low-carbon practices and disincentives (e.g., through taxes) for those that produce high emissions.

### 3. Data Transparency

Business actors are required to record and report the implementation of mitigation, adaptation, and carbon pricing actions in the SRN PPI. This creates transparency in emissions data and reduction efforts.

### 4. Financing Sources

Funds collected from carbon pricing (for example from carbon taxes or carbon trading) can be allocated to support:
- Climate change projects
- Sustainable development initiatives
- Investment in renewable energy

### 5. Multi-sector Collaboration

The implementation of carbon pricing involves various sectors:
- Energy
- Waste
- Industry
- Agriculture
- Forestry

This encourages collaboration between government, business actors, and the community in achieving emission reduction targets.

### 6. Monitoring, Reporting, and Verification (MRV)

The MRV system forms the primary data basis for carbon pricing governance, ensuring that emission reduction claims can be accurately measured, reported, and verified.

## Development of Carbon Pricing Mechanisms

### Important Foundations

Implementing carbon pricing mechanisms requires:

1. **Social Justice**: Equitable distribution of costs and benefits
2. **Proper Governance**: Clear institutional frameworks and decision-making processes
3. **Environmental Improvement**: Measurable environmental outcomes
4. **Strengthened Infrastructure**: Physical and digital systems for carbon market operations
5. **Credible Institutions**: Trusted regulatory and oversight bodies
6. **Robust MRV Systems**: Reliable monitoring, reporting, and verification

### New Economic Paradigm

Carbon pricing drives a transformation toward sustainability:

**Green Market → Green Investment → Green Jobs**

This creates a virtuous cycle where:
- Carbon pricing creates market demand for low-carbon solutions
- Investment flows to green technologies and projects
- New employment opportunities emerge in sustainable sectors

### Carbon Pricing Mechanism Types

#### 1. Carbon Trading
Market-based mechanism where emission allowances or credits can be bought and sold.

#### 2. Result-based Payment
Payment systems tied to verified emission reductions or carbon sequestration outcomes.

#### 3. Carbon Levy
Tax or fee imposed on carbon emissions, with other mechanisms aligned with science and technology.

### Alignment with Paris Agreement NDCs

Indonesia's carbon pricing mechanisms support three pathways:

1. **Result-based Payment**: Performance-based financing for emission reductions
2. **Compliance Market**: Mandatory carbon trading for regulated entities
3. **Voluntary Carbon Market**: Optional participation for entities seeking to offset emissions

## Carbon Pricing Update under UNFCCC

### Historical Timeline

#### 1992: Rio Earth Summit
- Introduced **Common But Differentiated Responsibilities and Respective Capabilities (CBDR-RC)**
- Foundation for international climate cooperation

#### 2015: The Paris Agreement
- Result of COP21 international agreement on climate change
- Introduced concept of climate justice in preamble
- Emphasized importance of agreement considering "being considered in any efforts to address climate change"

#### 2021: Glasgow Climate Pact
- Output of COP26
- Re-emphasized importance of climate justice
- Highlighted importance for some of the concept of climate justice, in the context of action to address climate change

#### 2022: COP27
- Agreed to provide special funding to address **loss and damages** (loss and damages fund) for developing countries from the Conference of the Parties in its twenty-seventh session

#### 2023: Paris Climate Finance Summit
- World Bank allowing low-income countries to temporarily suspend debt repayment if affected by climate disasters
- Providing financial assistance to climate-vulnerable countries

## Key Insights and Analysis

### Strengths

1. **Natural Advantage**: Indonesia's geography provides exceptional carbon sequestration potential, particularly through blue carbon ecosystems
2. **Comprehensive Framework**: Multi-layered governance structure involving national registry, exchange platforms, and international cooperation
3. **Sectoral Integration**: Carbon pricing spans all major emission sectors (FOLU, Energy, IPPU, Agriculture, Waste)
4. **International Alignment**: Strong commitment to Paris Agreement Article 6 mechanisms
5. **Bilateral Partnerships**: Active engagement with developed nations (Japan, Norway, Nordic countries)

### Challenges and Considerations

1. **Institutional Capacity**: Requires significant capacity building across multiple government agencies and private sector participants
2. **MRV System Robustness**: Critical need for reliable, transparent monitoring and verification systems
3. **Social Justice**: Ensuring equitable distribution of carbon pricing impacts across different socioeconomic groups
4. **Market Integrity**: Preventing double counting and ensuring environmental additionality
5. **Coordination Complexity**: Managing multiple bilateral agreements and international standards simultaneously

### Strategic Recommendations

1. **Accelerate MRV Infrastructure**: Invest in digital systems for real-time monitoring and transparent reporting
2. **Capacity Building**: Develop training programs for DNA, project developers, and verifiers
3. **Stakeholder Engagement**: Ensure meaningful participation from local communities, especially in FOLU projects
4. **Harmonization Priority**: Focus on mutual recognition agreements to facilitate international carbon credit trading
5. **Blue Carbon Development**: Leverage Indonesia's unique coastal and marine ecosystems as a competitive advantage
6. **Regional Leadership**: Position Indonesia as the ASEAN carbon market hub through regional cooperation frameworks

### Future Outlook

The Nusantara Code represents an ambitious and comprehensive approach to carbon pricing governance. Success will depend on:

- **Effective implementation** of the regulatory framework
- **Building trust** through transparent, credible systems
- **Balancing** economic development with environmental integrity
- **Ensuring** social justice and community benefits
- **Maintaining** international cooperation and standards alignment

Indonesia's carbon market development could serve as a model for other developing nations with significant natural carbon potential, demonstrating how to leverage environmental assets while contributing to global climate goals.
