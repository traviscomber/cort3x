# Cort3x — Design & Identity System

Status: CANONICAL DRAFT 1.0  
Owner: Product / Design / Engineering  
Applies to: `cort3x.app`, authenticated workspace, public product pages, presentations, product screenshots, email UI, generated reports and future native surfaces.

---

## 0. Why this document exists

Cort3x is not an AI coaching site, a generic SaaS dashboard, an incubator template, or a consulting brochure.

Cort3x is an **Innovation Intelligence OS**: a system that turns fragmented research and operational evidence into traceable opportunities, decisions and execution.

The identity must make that visible before the user reads an explanation.

The product should feel:

- quiet, not empty;
- intelligent, not futuristic for its own sake;
- technical, not developer-centric;
- premium, not luxurious;
- evidence-led, not decorative;
- operational, not dashboard-generic;
- related to N3uralia, but unmistakably Cort3x.

The visual system is built around one idea:

> **Trace the path from evidence to decision.**

Every major visual decision should reinforce traceability, structure, confidence and action.

## 1. Product identity

### Category

**Innovation Intelligence OS**

### Core promise

Cort3x converts scattered research, market signals, technology intelligence and internal evidence into a persistent decision system.

### Canonical product loop

`RESEARCH → EVIDENCE → SIGNAL → OPPORTUNITY → DECISION → EXECUTION → LEARNING`

This loop is the primary narrative and information architecture. It is not a decorative process diagram.

### Product behavior

Cort3x should answer six questions quickly:

1. What changed?
2. What evidence supports it?
3. What signal does it create?
4. What opportunity or risk does it imply?
5. What decision is pending or already made?
6. What happens next?

### Identity statement

**Cort3x is the operating layer between research and action.**

### Brand attributes

Primary attributes:

- precise;
- calm;
- analytical;
- traceable;
- decisive;
- editorial;
- operational.

Secondary attributes:

- global;
- technical;
- contemporary;
- restrained;
- human-in-the-loop.

Never target these attributes:

- playful;
- glossy;
- crypto-like;
- neon cyberpunk;
- startup-cute;
- consultancy-corporate;
- dashboard-busy.

## 2. Relationship to N3uralia

Cort3x belongs to the N3uralia product family.

It inherits:

- near-black foundations;
- restrained cool accents;
- technical typography;
- strong negative space;
- thin rules and indexing;
- evidence-first interface behavior;
- compact copy;
- minimal decorative UI.

It does **not** copy the N3uralia landing page or N3uralia360 composition.

Cort3x is more analytical and decision-oriented. Its visual metaphor is not immersion; it is **traceability**.

N3uralia should feel like the intelligence infrastructure company.
Cort3x should feel like the intelligence operating system built for innovation decisions.

## 3. The Cort3x visual idea: TRACE

The core graphic language is `TRACE`.

A trace is a visible relationship between:

- source and evidence;
- evidence and signal;
- signal and opportunity;
- opportunity and decision;
- decision and execution;
- execution and outcome.

### Graphic primitives

Use only a small family of primitives:

- point;
- thin line;
- bracket;
- axis;
- index number;
- rectangular field;
- cropped image/evidence frame.

These primitives can form diagrams, status rails, timelines, source maps, decision chains and editorial compositions.

### Geometry

Preferred:

- 1 px rules;
- squared or nearly squared corners;
- asymmetric grids;
- anchored labels;
- visible alignment;
- controlled cropping;
- deliberate empty zones.

Avoid:

- floating blobs;
- pill-shaped containers as the default;
- excessive rounded cards;
- gradient meshes;
- decorative orbit animations;
- abstract AI sparkles as a primary motif;
- glassmorphism;
- soft shadow-heavy surfaces.

### Signature motif

The canonical Cort3x motif is a **decision trace**:

`●────────┬────────□`

The point represents a source/evidence event. The junction represents interpretation or comparison. The rectangle represents a decision or action boundary.

This motif may be abstracted, rotated, cropped or expanded into larger information maps, but it should always feel structural rather than ornamental.

## 4. Logo system

### Existing mark

The existing Cort3x symbol may remain as the transitional product mark while the full identity is rolled out. It should no longer be presented inside oversized rounded-square badges except where platform icon requirements demand it.

### Canonical lockups

Three lockups are permitted:

1. `Cort3x` wordmark only.
2. Symbol + `Cort3x`.
3. `Cort3x` + small endorsement `by N3uralia`.

Do not use `powered by n3uralia` as the primary product name.

Preferred endorsement:

`Cort3x`  
`BY N3URALIA`

The endorsement is always secondary and visually quiet.

### Clear space

Minimum clear space around the mark: the cap height of the `C` in Cort3x.

### Logo color

Primary dark application:

- wordmark: `C3X-BONE`;
- symbol: `C3X-SIGNAL` or `C3X-BONE`;
- endorsement: `C3X-MIST`.

Primary light application:

- wordmark: `C3X-INK`;
- symbol: `C3X-MOSS`;
- endorsement: `C3X-GRAPHITE`.

Never:

- add shadows;
- add gradients;
- stretch the mark;
- place it in a decorative capsule;
- combine it with an AI sparkle icon;
- use multiple accent colors inside one mark.

## 5. Color system

Cort3x is dark-first. Light mode is supported for documents, exports and selected dense data surfaces, but the canonical product identity is dark.

### Core palette

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---:|---|
| `C3X-VOID` | `#070A09` | primary background |
| `C3X-INK` | `#0D1110` | raised dark surface |
| `C3X-GRAPHITE` | `#171D1B` | secondary surface |
| `C3X-RULE` | `#29322F` | borders, axes, separators |
| `C3X-MIST` | `#8F9A96` | secondary text |
| `C3X-BONE` | `#E7E9E2` | primary text |
| `C3X-WHITE` | `#F7F8F4` | rare high-emphasis text |
| `C3X-MOSS` | `#8C9861` | brand anchor / selected state |
| `C3X-SIGNAL` | `#79B7B1` | evidence, links, active trace |
| `C3X-AMBER` | `#C79A57` | caution / decision pending |
| `C3X-RED` | `#C96A64` | critical / destructive only |
| `C3X-GREEN` | `#6FA47E` | verified / positive only |

### Brand accent hierarchy

Use accents in this order:

1. `C3X-MOSS` — identity and selection.
2. `C3X-SIGNAL` — evidence and trace relationships.
3. semantic colors — only for real state.

Do not use amber or red as general decoration.

### Color proportion

Typical dark screen:

- 70–80% `VOID / INK`;
- 15–20% `BONE / MIST / RULE`;
- 5–8% `MOSS / SIGNAL`;
- <2% semantic alert color.

### Light surfaces

Light mode / report palette:

- background: `#F2F3EE`;
- surface: `#F8F9F6`;
- primary text: `#141817`;
- secondary text: `#5C6662`;
- rule: `#D4D9D5`;
- moss: `#6F7B4B`;
- signal: `#4F8F8B`.

### No gradients

Cort3x does not use decorative gradients.

A subtle photographic overlay or opacity mask is allowed when needed for legibility, but it must read as image treatment rather than brand gradient.

## 6. Typography

### Type families

Canonical stack:

- Display / section headers: **IBM Plex Sans Condensed**.
- Body / interface: **Inter**.
- Data / IDs / timestamps / source references: **JetBrains Mono**.

If IBM Plex Sans Condensed is not yet installed, continue with Inter temporarily; do not substitute a decorative display font.

### Typography behavior

Display typography is technical and editorial, not loud.

Use:

- uppercase for micro-labels and short section identifiers;
- sentence case for interface actions;
- restrained tracking;
- medium-to-large headings with deliberate line breaks;
- mono only where data semantics benefit from it.

Avoid:

- ultra-light body text;
- bold-everything hierarchy;
- all-caps paragraphs;
- tiny low-contrast labels;
- novelty geometric fonts in operational UI.

### Type scale

| Role | Size | Weight | Line height |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero display | `clamp(3rem, 7vw, 7rem)` | 400 | 0.94–1.00 |
| Page title | `clamp(2rem, 4vw, 4rem)` | 400 | 1.00–1.08 |
| Section title | `clamp(1.5rem, 2.5vw, 2.5rem)` | 400 | 1.10 |
| H3 | 1.25rem | 500 | 1.25 |
| Body large | 1.125rem | 400 | 1.55 |
| Body | 1rem | 400 | 1.55 |
| UI | 0.875rem | 500 | 1.35 |
| Micro | 0.6875–0.75rem | 500 | 1.25 |
| Mono data | 0.75–0.875rem | 400/500 | 1.35 |

### Line length

Body copy: 52–72 characters per line.

Operational tables and panels may be denser, but long-form text must not span full viewport width.

## 7. Spacing and grid

### Base unit

Base spacing unit: `4px`.

Primary spacing steps:

`4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 48 / 64 / 96 / 128`

### Public grid

Desktop:

- 12 columns;
- max content width: 1440 px;
- minimum page gutter: 32 px;
- preferred gutter: 48–64 px on large screens.

Mobile:

- 4 columns;
- 20 px side gutter;
- preserve large vertical rhythm;
- collapse complexity instead of shrinking everything.

### App grid

Authenticated workspace uses three structural zones:

1. navigation rail;
2. primary work area;
3. contextual intelligence / evidence rail when needed.

The third rail is conditional. Do not reserve empty chrome when there is no contextual content.

### Density

Public pages: low-to-medium density.

Command Center: medium density.

Research / evidence workspace: medium-to-high density with strong hierarchy.

Tables: dense but breathable.

## 8. Shape, radius and border rules

### Radius

Canonical radii:

- `0px` — diagrams, tables, large framing regions;
- `2px` — buttons, inputs, compact controls;
- `4px` — panels where touch/visual softness is useful;
- `8px` — exceptional media containers only.

Do not use 12–24 px rounded SaaS cards as the default.

### Borders

Default rule: `1px solid C3X-RULE`.

Borders should describe structure, not decorate every container.

Prefer:

- open layouts with separator rules;
- one enclosing frame over nested frames;
- alignment and spacing before borders.

### Shadows

Default: none.

Use a subtle shadow only for floating menus, dialogs or temporary overlays that need physical separation.

---

## 9. Surface system

There are only four surface levels:

1. `Canvas` — full page background.
2. `Section` — large structural grouping, often borderless.
3. `Panel` — interactive or data grouping.
4. `Overlay` — modal, menu, tooltip.

Do not create card-inside-card hierarchies.

A panel must exist because it groups interaction, data, state or context — not because content needs a box.

---

## 10. Iconography

### Style

Use a single family of thin, geometric line icons.

Rules:

- stroke: 1.5–1.75 px;
- square optical box;
- no filled cartoon icons;
- no multicolor icons;
- no decorative 3D symbols;
- no AI sparkle as generic shorthand for intelligence.

### Domain symbols

Cort3x domain objects should have stable symbols:

- Research — scan / source field;
- Evidence — document / quoted source;
- Signal — pulse / breakpoint;
- Opportunity — opening / directional gate;
- Decision — checked junction / commit node;
- Execution — forward rail / task sequence;
- Learning — feedback loop / return trace.

The symbol should reinforce the semantic object, not imitate a consumer app icon.

---

## 11. Imagery

### Public product imagery

Preferred:

- real operational environments;
- research source material;
- product screenshots;
- annotated evidence;
- maps, documents, patents, market material, technical drawings;
- controlled black-and-white or desaturated photography.

Treat photography as evidence, not mood wallpaper.

### Image treatment

- crop decisively;
- avoid generic stock-office scenes;
- use low saturation;
- permit subtle moss/cyan indexing overlays;
- no rounded floating image cards;
- captions should identify source/context when relevant.

### AI-generated imagery

Allowed for conceptual explanation only.

Never use generated imagery to imply a real customer, deployment, partner or outcome.

---

## 12. Data visualization

Cort3x charts exist to support decisions.

Preferred chart hierarchy:

1. direct value + delta;
2. ranked list;
3. timeline;
4. bar / dot plot;
5. line chart;
6. matrix / quadrant;
7. network or relationship view only when relationships matter.

Avoid pie and donut charts unless the part-to-whole relationship is genuinely important.

### Chart color

Use neutral series first.
Highlight one important series with `MOSS` or `SIGNAL`.
Use semantic colors only for actual states.

### Chart labels

Prefer direct labels over legends where possible.
Always expose units, period and source.

---

## 13. Motion

Motion communicates causality and state.

Allowed:

- 120–180 ms control transitions;
- 180–260 ms panel transitions;
- subtle line/trace drawing when a relationship is created;
- progressive reveal for evidence chains;
- loading skeletons with restrained motion;
- scroll reveal on public pages when it does not delay reading.

Avoid:

- infinite decorative movement;
- bouncing CTA buttons;
- cursor gimmicks;
- large parallax that compromises usability;
- animated gradients;
- motion used to make static cards feel “AI”.

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.

## 14. Voice and language

### Voice

Cort3x is direct, factual and calm.

Write in this order:

1. what was observed;
2. what it means;
3. confidence / uncertainty;
4. what decision or action is required.

### Preferred verbs

`capture`  
`trace`  
`compare`  
`structure`  
`verify`  
`prioritize`  
`decide`  
`assign`  
`execute`  
`learn`

### Avoid

- “revolutionary”;
- “game-changing”;
- “super accelerator”;
- “powered by cutting-edge AI”;
- “months of work in seconds” without evidence;
- unsupported accuracy / ROI / adoption claims;
- generic “unlock your potential” language.

### Product naming

Canonical terms:

- Research
- Evidence
- Signal
- Opportunity
- Decision
- Initiative
- Execution
- Outcome
- Source
- Confidence
- Owner
- Next action

Do not alternate between synonyms for the same object within one workflow.

### Language consistency

One language per flow.

English, Spanish and Indonesian can all be supported, but UI strings, system states and CTA labels must not mix languages within a screen.

## 16. Authenticated experience

The app is a command system, not a marketing continuation.

### Canonical navigation

`COMMAND CENTER`

`RESEARCH`

`SIGNALS`

`OPPORTUNITIES`

`DECISIONS`

`PORTFOLIO`

`EXECUTION`

`KNOWLEDGE`

Secondary/system:

`ADMIN` — permission-gated and visually separate.

`SETTINGS`

### Command Center

The first screen should answer:

- what changed;
- what requires attention;
- what decisions are pending;
- what evidence is new;
- which initiatives are drifting;
- what action should happen next.

Do not lead with decorative KPI cards.

Preferred composition:

- left: current priorities / decision queue;
- center: signal and evidence timeline;
- right: exceptions / owners / next actions;
- lower region: portfolio health and traceability gaps.

### Initiative workspace

Primary hierarchy:

1. Current thesis / context.
2. Evidence.
3. Signals.
4. Opportunities / risks.
5. Decisions.
6. Execution.
7. Outcomes.
8. Operational metadata.

Progress percentage and budget are secondary, not the narrative header.

## 17. AI interaction model

AI is an intelligence layer, not a mascot.

### AI assistant behavior

Every substantive answer should distinguish:

- observed evidence;
- derived signal;
- inference;
- confidence;
- recommendation;
- required human decision.

### AI UI

The assistant can appear as:

- contextual side panel;
- command palette action;
- inline evidence synthesis;
- compare / challenge action;
- decision brief generator.

Avoid a permanent decorative floating sparkle button as the primary intelligence affordance.

### Provenance

AI-generated claims shown inside the product should provide a visible route back to source material whenever possible.

## 18. Core components

### Navigation rail

- dark canvas;
- compact width;
- 14–16 px icons;
- active state uses moss rule / field, not bright fill;
- labels remain readable;
- collapse on small screens.

### Top context bar

Use only when the workspace needs context such as initiative, geography, time range, search or command actions.

Do not duplicate global navigation.

### Buttons

Primary:

- rectangular;
- `MOSS` fill on dark surfaces;
- dark text;
- 2 px radius;
- 40–44 px minimum height.

Secondary:

- transparent;
- 1 px rule;
- bone text.

Tertiary:

- text action with directional indicator.

Destructive:

- red only at action point, not on entire container.

### Inputs

- flat field;
- 1 px border;
- clear focus ring;
- labels above field;
- visible helper/error text;
- no floating labels.

### Panels

- one structural purpose;
- title + optional status + action;
- no unnecessary icon bubbles;
- no nested panel unless function demands it.

### Tables

- sticky header when long;
- visible row focus/hover;
- IDs and dates may use mono;
- align numeric data right;
- bulk actions appear only after selection.

### Badges

Use badges only for state or classification.

Do not use pills to decorate every metadata value.

### Empty states

Every empty state should say:

- what is missing;
- why it matters;
- how to create/import it;
- whether the absence is expected or a data problem.

### Loading

Prefer structural skeletons.
Do not show fake percentages unless progress is measurable.

### Errors

Expose:

- human-readable failure;
- what remained safe;
- retry path;
- escalation or support path when appropriate.

---

## 19. Accessibility

Required baseline:

- WCAG AA contrast minimum;
- keyboard-reachable interactive UI;
- visible focus state;
- correct landmarks and heading order;
- semantic labels for icons;
- no color-only status communication;
- minimum 44 px touch target on mobile;
- reduced-motion support;
- no horizontal overflow on supported breakpoints;
- charts must expose textual equivalents for key findings.

Operational density is not an excuse for inaccessible text.

---

## 20. Responsive behavior

### Desktop

Use space to expose relationships and context.

### Tablet

Collapse the contextual rail before reducing primary workspace width below comfortable reading/interaction size.

### Mobile

Mobile is not compressed desktop.

Priority order:

1. current context;
2. next action;
3. critical state;
4. evidence / decision content;
5. secondary analytics.

Large comparison tables may become stacked records or horizontal data views only when scrolling is explicit and usable.

## 22. Anti-patterns — explicitly deprecated

The following patterns are not part of the Cort3x identity:

- oversized rounded green pills;
- generic `AI + Coaching` positioning;
- login form embedded in hero;
- multi-column feature-card walls;
- logo carousels implying unverified trust;
- gold/orange used as general premium decoration;
- 12 px+ radius on most cards;
- AI sparkle icons used as generic intelligence symbol;
- white backgrounds as the dominant product mode;
- gradient hero backgrounds;
- floating blobs / abstract 3D shapes;
- excessive drop shadows;
- mixed Spanish / English in one screen;
- KPIs presented without source, period or decision relevance;
- progress percentage as the primary description of an initiative;
- cards containing cards containing cards.

## 23. Migration from current UI

The current codebase uses a light-first token set with olive primary, gold secondary, orange accent and a large `0.75rem` radius. This is transitional and should not be expanded.

Migration order:

### Phase 1 — foundation

- replace global color tokens;
- add typography tokens;
- reduce radius system;
- define rule/border behavior;
- standardize button/input states;
- normalize focus states.

### Phase 2 — shell

- rebuild public navigation;
- rebuild authenticated app shell;
- remove decorative AI bubble treatment;
- establish command/context architecture.

### Phase 3 — core product

- Command Center;
- Initiative workspace;
- Research / Evidence;
- Signals;
- Opportunities;
- Decisions;
- Execution.

### Phase 4 — public product story

- new landing;
- product proof sections;
- use cases;
- product walkthrough;
- pricing/contact only if commercially required.

### Phase 5 — cleanup

- retire legacy marketing imagery;
- retire unverified logo/trust surfaces;
- remove obsolete components and token variants after reachability is verified;
- verify responsive and accessibility across all canonical surfaces.

## 24. Quality gate

A Cort3x screen is not complete until all are true:

- its primary decision/action is clear in under five seconds;
- it reads as Cort3x without relying on the logo;
- evidence and source context are visible where relevant;
- semantic status colors represent real state;
- no unnecessary frame or card can be removed without losing structure;
- spacing follows the canonical scale;
- typography hierarchy uses the defined roles;
- mobile composition is intentional;
- keyboard and focus behavior work;
- loading, empty, error and permission states exist;
- no unsupported claim appears;
- no mixed-language UI appears;
- no horizontal overflow exists;
- visual QA has been performed on the rendered product.

---

## 25. Design review scorecard

Every major surface is scored 0–100 across:

- Brand coherence;
- UI clarity;
- UX clarity;
- Accessibility;
- System consistency;
- Evidence / provenance visibility;
- Decision usefulness;
- Premium quality.

Release target for canonical surfaces: **≥ 90 overall**, with no category below 85.

A high score cannot compensate for a P0/P1 issue in security, permissions, accessibility or core workflow.

---

## 26. Canonical one-line test

When evaluating any design decision, ask:

> **Does this make the path from evidence to decision easier to see, trust and act on?**

If not, remove it.

## 27. Governance

`DESIGN.md` is the canonical design source for Cort3x.

Rules:

- shared tokens change here before being expanded across the product;
- new patterns must reuse existing primitives unless a new need is demonstrated;
- deviations require an explicit product or accessibility reason;
- screenshots are not design specifications; implemented behavior is;
- legacy UI remains legacy until migrated — it does not redefine this system;
- product claims must be supportable by real evidence;
- design implementation must preserve auth, RLS, data provenance and operational truth.
